What OpenSplit is for

You take a trip, share a flat, or just split a dinner. People pay for things at different times, in different amounts, and sometimes in different currencies. Remembering who covered what stops being possible after about the third day.

OpenSplit keeps that record. You log each expense — who paid, how much, and how it should be divided — and it works out who owes whom, and the smallest set of payments that settles everybody up.

  • Make a group for a trip, a flat, or a single evening out.
  • Add an expense and split it evenly, by exact amounts, by shares, or by percentage.
  • See balances per currency — never silently netted across one, because an exchange rate nobody agreed to is not a balance.
  • Invite people with a link. People who do not have the app can still be in a group, so nobody has to install anything to be included in the maths.
  • Settle up, and see how each figure came to be what it is.

Everything works with no connection at all — the full record lives on your device, so travelling abroad is the main use case rather than an edge case. Your groups sync between your own devices and the people you share a group with once you are back online.

Why OpenSplit asks you to sign in with Google

Signing in is what lets your groups exist on more than one device, and lets the people in a group see the same figures you do.

Google sign-in is one of three ways in, alongside an email address or continuing as a guest. If you choose it, OpenSplit asks Google for the basic sign-in scopes only — your name and email address. That is used to identify your account when you sign in again or on another device, and to show your name to the people in your groups.

OpenSplit never receives your Google password. It does not request, and cannot read, your Gmail, Drive, Calendar, Contacts, photos or any other Google data.

What OpenSplit does not do

  • No ads and no tracking. There is no analytics SDK in the app, no advertising identifier, and no location or contacts access.
  • Nothing is sold. Not your data, not your expenses, not your address.
  • No money changes hands here. OpenSplit records who owes whom. It never handles, holds, moves or processes money, and is not a payment service. If you add a UPI ID, it is so somebody can open their own payment app to pay you.
  • Logging an expense is never gated. No caps, no limits, no upsell at the moment you are trying to record a bill.

Where to get it

OpenSplit runs in any modern browser at opensplit.web.app/app, and as an Android app.

It is open source — the whole client and server live at github.com/eigeninteractive/opensplit — and the server side is genuinely self-hostable: it stores rows and enforces one arithmetic invariant, and computes nothing about your money on its own.

OpenSplit is in active development ahead of its first public release.
